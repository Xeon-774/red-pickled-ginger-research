# Experimental protocol: sodium reduction and low-sodium DIY

This protocol supports issue #5. It is designed to prevent literature values from other foods being silently treated as red-ginger measurements.

## Experiment A — rinsing / soaking

### Material

Use one commercial product lot. Preferred first target: the low-sodium 1 kg Gyomu Super product if locally available.

### Arms

Prepare four equal 50 g drained portions.

- A0: no rinse
- A1: gentle water rinse for 30 s
- A2: soak in 500 mL cold water for 5 min
- A3: soak in 500 mL cold water for 30 min

Drain each treated sample for exactly 2 min.

### Measurements

For every arm record:

- initial drained mass
- final drained mass
- apparent salinity using the same instrument/procedure
- pH
- blind crispness score 1-5
- blind saltiness score 1-5
- photo

If only a consumer salinity/conductivity meter is available, report results as **apparent salinity**, not sodium concentration.

### Decision rule

A treatment is useful if it materially reduces measured apparent salinity without unacceptable loss of crispness.

Do not convert external takuan/canned-food percentages into the result.

## Experiment B — low-sodium acidified DIY

### Objective

Find the lowest-sodium formulation that:

- tastes acceptable;
- remains crisp enough;
- reaches measured pH <=4.0;
- can be kept refrigerated.

### Starting design

Use 100-200 g batches.

Hold ginger geometry constant. Use ordinary brewed vinegar as the dominant acid source and red shiso / approved natural color for color.

Test salt as a sensory variable, for example:

- B0: no added salt
- B1: 0.5% added salt by ginger mass
- B2: 1.0%
- B3: 2.0%

These are **experimental flavor levels, not preservation guarantees**.

### Safety controls

- Wash raw ginger thoroughly.
- Use clean, sanitized utensils and containers.
- Keep refrigerated at <=10°C.
- Measure pH after the product has equilibrated; do not infer pH from vinegar volume.
- Target pH <=4.0.
- Do not use room-temperature storage.
- Do not make a long shelf-life claim from this experiment.

### Measurements

- raw ginger mass
- finished drained mass
- vinegar volume
- colorant / shiso mass
- salt mass
- pH at 24 h and later checkpoints
- apparent salinity or analytical sodium
- crispness score
- acid/salt flavor score
- total ingredient cost

### Output

Once measured, add actual rows to the cost matrix using:

`finished JPY/100g = total batch ingredient cost / finished drained mass g * 100`

and keep ingredient-input cost as a separate field.
