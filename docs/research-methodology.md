# Research methodology

## Primary objectives

Find red pickled ginger options that minimize total cost while preserving acceptable food-safety evidence, low sodium where feasible, and practical quality.

## User preference model

- Daily side-dish use
- Initial consumption: about 300 g/month
- Bulk purchase acceptable
- Freezing acceptable
- Chinese / other imported origins acceptable
- Natural coloring acceptable
- Synthetic coloring is not automatically excluded if exposure is demonstrably low-risk, but uncertainty is penalized
- MSG acceptable
- pH regulators / acidulants acceptable if safety is established
- Allergies and individual hypersensitivity are out of scope
- Shredded form preferred
- Crisp texture preferred but is a secondary variable
- Local retail scope: Hadano City, Kanagawa
- DIY acceptable if effort remains reasonable

## Quantitative scenarios

| Scenario | Intake |
|---|---:|
| Normal comparison | 10 g/day |
| High intake | 50 g/day |
| Stress test | 100 g/day |

Reference body mass: 70 kg.

For additives with a numeric ADI:

- calculate the ADI for 70 kg;
- calculate 10% of that ADI as a conservative single-food allocation;
- compare candidate exposure at 50 g/day and 100 g/day;
- if actual concentration is unavailable, use a legal maximum only when it applies directly and is documented;
- otherwise classify exposure as unresolved.

## Sodium interpretation

Preferred threshold:

- <=3 g salt equivalent / 100 g: strong
- >3 to 5 g / 100 g: moderate
- >5 g / 100 g: high

Rinsed / soaked values must never be inferred without measured or sourced data.

## Price normalization

Record separately:

- package price
- package net mass
- solid mass if different from net mass
- shipping
- effective delivered price
- JPY / 100 g edible solids
- bulk break-even based on realistic consumption and shelf life

## Evidence rules

Preferred source order:

1. manufacturer / brand official product page
2. Japanese government / regulator / international risk-assessment body
3. major retailer product page with full label image or transcription
4. secondary retailer listing
5. anecdotal sources only for taste / texture context

Every candidate should distinguish:

- verified
- calculated
- estimated
- unknown

Do not convert a hazard report into a practical risk conclusion without dose and exposure context.
